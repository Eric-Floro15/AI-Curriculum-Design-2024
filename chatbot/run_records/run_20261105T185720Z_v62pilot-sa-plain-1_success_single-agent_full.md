## Caveats and Trade-offs

1. **Cluster 5 stability caveat.** Cluster 5 is the largest cluster (370 skills) but also the least stable (median confidence 0.47, 195 of 370 skills below 0.5). Many skills in this cluster co-cluster most strongly with Cluster 3 (Data Engineering). The course-to-cluster mapping above should be treated as thematic guidance, not hard partitioning — in practice, Courses 1, 7, and 10 draw heavily from both Clusters 3 and 5 simultaneously.

2. **Lift is conditional on the focal skill.** All lift/z values cited above were computed with **Agentic AI** as the focal skill. A programme focused on, say, healthcare AI or quantitative finance would generate a different set of distinctively co-demanded skills. The agentic-AI pivot is empirically justified by the Winter 2026 data and the industry reports, but professors targeting a specific sector should additionally run a sector-specific wrap analysis before finalising the elective mix.

3. **Cluster 6 (Business Intelligence) is under-weighted.** This programme places Cluster 6 skills primarily in the Capstone (Course 10). If the target employer base skews toward analytics-heavy roles (data scientist, analytics engineer), a dedicated Business Intelligence & Analytics course covering Power BI, Excel, Data Visualization, and Dashboard Development (Cluster 6's top skills by market frequency) would be warranted.

4. **The taxonomy is a point-in-time snapshot.** All cluster frequencies and lift values derive from the Winter 2026 wave of job-postings. Skill demand in the agentic AI space is moving quickly — LangGraph, CrewAI, and AutoGen all appear at high lift but relatively low absolute frequency (< 1% of all postings each), meaning a single major framework shift could alter their relative standing within one or two semesters. We recommend revisiting the lift table annually at minimum.
