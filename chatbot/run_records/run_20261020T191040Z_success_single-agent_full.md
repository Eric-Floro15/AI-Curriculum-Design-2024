## Trade-offs and Caveats

1. **Lift figures condition on Agentic AI as the focal skill.** The lift/z values cited throughout (e.g. LangGraph lift 12.25×, z=6.83) measure co-demand *specifically within Agentic AI job postings*. Skills like Python (38.3% frequency, Cluster 3) and Statistics (13.5%, Cluster 7) are absolutely essential at population scale but happen not to be *distinctively* concentrated in agentic postings — they should not be deprioritised on that basis. The programme uses both metrics: lift/z to justify the *shape* of the emerging-skills courses (3, 4, 7), and market frequency to justify the breadth foundations (courses 1, 6, 8).

2. **Cluster labels are a point-in-time partition.** The CSPA ensemble clustering reflects Winter 2026 job postings. Skills span clusters (e.g. Kubernetes appears in both Cluster 2 and Cluster 3); the course-to-cluster mapping above is a primary assignment, not a hard boundary.

3. **Heterogeneous clusters (6, 8, 9) are disclosed but not themed.** Cluster 9 in particular — containing tools like NVIDIA NIM and Segment Anything Model (SAM) — merits monitoring as a leading indicator of the next wave of niche tooling, but its current posting frequency is too low to anchor a course.

4. **Peer programme data reflects verified snapshots (as of June 2026).** Programme structures at CMU, Stanford, Georgia Tech, and Queen's change year-to-year; the URLs above should be consulted for the most current requirements before finalising course equivalencies or articulation agreements.

5. **No sector wrap applied.** This curriculum is designed as a general-purpose AI/ML graduate programme. A sector-specific variant (e.g. healthcare, finance) would require an additional sector-wrap analysis to layer domain-distinctive skills on top of this core scaffold.

## Trade-offs and Caveats

1. **Lift figures condition on Agentic AI as the focal skill.** The lift/z values cited throughout (e.g. LangGraph lift 12.25×, z=6.83) measure co-demand *specifically within Agentic AI job postings*. Skills like Python (38.3% frequency, Cluster 3) and Statistics (13.5%, Cluster 7) are absolutely essential at population scale but happen not to be *distinctively* concentrated in agentic postings — they should not be deprioritised on that basis. The programme uses both metrics: lift/z to justify the *shape* of the emerging-skills courses (3, 4, 7), and market frequency to justify the breadth foundations (courses 1, 6, 8).

2. **Cluster labels are a point-in-time partition.** The CSPA ensemble clustering reflects Winter 2026 job postings. Skills span clusters (e.g. Kubernetes appears in both Cluster 2 and Cluster 3); the course-to-cluster mapping above is a primary assignment, not a hard boundary.

3. **Heterogeneous clusters (6, 8, 9) are disclosed but not themed.** Cluster 9 in particular — containing tools like NVIDIA NIM and Segment Anything Model (SAM) — merits monitoring as a leading indicator of the next wave of niche tooling, but its current posting frequency is too low to anchor a course.

4. **Peer programme data reflects verified snapshots (as of June 2026).** Programme structures at CMU, Stanford, Georgia Tech, and Queen's change year-to-year; the URLs above should be consulted for the most current requirements before finalising course equivalencies or articulation agreements.

5. **No sector wrap applied.** This curriculum is designed as a general-purpose AI/ML graduate programme. A sector-specific variant (e.g. healthcare, finance) would require an additional sector-wrap analysis to layer domain-distinctive skills on top of this core scaffold.
