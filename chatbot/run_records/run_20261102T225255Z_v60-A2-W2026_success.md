## Trade-Offs & Caveats the Professor Should Consider

1. **Cluster 2 spans three courses (2, 3, 4).** With 131 skills and median confidence 0.88, this cluster is richly dense — splitting it into ML foundations, Deep Learning, and Generative AI is empirically justified by the skill sub-groups' internal coherence, but it concentrates ~30% of the program on a single cluster's domain. If the program must be compressed to 6–7 courses, Courses 2 and 3 could merge (at the cost of depth in computer vision and NLP).

2. **Cluster 5 is intentionally diffuse.** With 370 skills and median confidence 0.47, Cluster 5 supplies skills to three different courses (5, 6, 7). There is no clean single-course mapping — this is a feature of the data, not an oversight. Skills like Docker (lift 4.73×, z=3.68) genuinely span MLOps, cloud platform, and agentic deployment contexts.

3. **The Agentic AI course (Course 5) is the highest-velocity and most volatile part of this curriculum.** Multi-agent system frameworks (CrewAI, AutoGen, LangGraph) are moving extremely rapidly — the lift/z signals are very high now but the specific tool landscape may look different in 18–24 months. This course needs the most frequent review and the most tool-agnostic framing (teach the pattern: orchestration, memory, tool-use, evaluation — then illustrate with current tools).

4. **Soft skills (Cluster 7) and project delivery (Cluster 4) have NO distinctive lift signal** — they are universally demanded, appearing in every AI/ML posting without distinguishing any sub-cluster. That is itself the finding: they are non-negotiable table-stakes that must be woven throughout every course as graduate program-wide outcomes, not isolated into a dedicated "professional skills" course that students can disengage from.

5. **This curriculum is grounded in Winter 2026 CSPA data.** The field evolves quickly. Annual re-anchoring of the course map against updated cluster data — especially for Courses 4 and 5 (GenAI + Agentic) — is strongly recommended.
