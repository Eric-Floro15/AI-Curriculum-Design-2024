## 6. Trade-offs and Caveats the Professor Should Consider

1. **Fixed-sequence constraint is real.** The MMAI is a fully fixed 12-month cohort program with no electives. Adding two new courses either displaces existing content or extends the program. The least disruptive path is to absorb data-engineering content into the existing *Deep Learning* course (which references "big data technologies" but underdelivers) and restructure *Machine Learning and AI Technology* into two courses — one for modelling, one for engineering — rather than adding entirely new slots.

2. **Tool obsolescence risk in fast-moving domains.** The engineering courses (MLflow, Kubeflow, LangChain, etc.) teach specific tools whose market share can shift in 12–18 months. Design the courses around *concepts* (model versioning, pipeline orchestration, observability) with specific tools as lab vehicles — not the other way around. This also applies to the finance variant: Azure Data Factory is dominant now in Canadian banks but should not be the conceptual anchor.

3. **Cluster 5 is heterogeneous.** The Cluster Interpreter notes that Cluster 5 (Core AI, Cloud & Data-Platform Engineering, 370 skills) has median confidence 0.47 — many individual skill placements within it are unstable. The gap finding is valid, but the cluster boundary is porous. Treat the missing skills from this cluster as thematically related, not as a single monolithic "engineering" block.

4. **Finance corpus gap in news research.** The AI Industry News Researcher found no dedicated finance-AI articles in the indexed corpus. The finance-specialisation rationale above is grounded in market lift/z data and adjacent industry signals — not in direct finance-sector news coverage. The professor should seek additional validation from finance-sector sources before finalising that variant.

## 6. Trade-offs and Caveats the Professor Should Consider

1. **Fixed-sequence constraint is real.** The MMAI is a fully fixed 12-month cohort program with no electives. Adding two new courses either displaces existing content or extends the program. The least disruptive path is to absorb data-engineering content into the existing *Deep Learning* course (which references "big data technologies" but underdelivers) and restructure *Machine Learning and AI Technology* into two courses — one for modelling, one for engineering — rather than adding entirely new slots.

2. **Tool obsolescence risk in fast-moving domains.** The engineering courses (MLflow, Kubeflow, LangChain, etc.) teach specific tools whose market share can shift in 12–18 months. Design the courses around *concepts* (model versioning, pipeline orchestration, observability) with specific tools as lab vehicles — not the other way around. This also applies to the finance variant: Azure Data Factory is dominant now in Canadian banks but should not be the conceptual anchor.

3. **Cluster 5 is heterogeneous.** The Cluster Interpreter notes that Cluster 5 (Core AI, Cloud & Data-Platform Engineering, 370 skills) has median confidence 0.47 — many individual skill placements within it are unstable. The gap finding is valid, but the cluster boundary is porous. Treat the missing skills from this cluster as thematically related, not as a single monolithic "engineering" block.

4. **Finance corpus gap in news research.** The AI Industry News Researcher found no dedicated finance-AI articles in the indexed corpus. The finance-specialisation rationale above is grounded in market lift/z data and adjacent industry signals — not in direct finance-sector news coverage. The professor should seek additional validation from finance-sector sources before finalising that variant.

5. **Syllabi not public.** The University AI Programs Researcher notes that Queen's MMAI does not publish week-by-week syllabi publicly. If individual courses embed more technical tool content than their descriptions suggest (e.g., SQL exercises within *ML & AI Technology*, Databricks labs within *Deep Learning*), some partial-coverage verdicts may need revision upon internal review.
