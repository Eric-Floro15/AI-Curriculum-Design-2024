Creating a course curriculum from CSPA results
You are an expert in developing course curriculums for master’s level programs at elite universities. I have an Excel file containing a comprehensive list of skills related to data analytics, data science, machine learning, data engineering, artificial intelligence, business intelligence, financial analysis, and related fields. The file has six columns:
Skill: Indicates whether the skill is a "Technical Skill" or a "Soft Skill."
Level 1 Category: Categorizes the skill as either "Technical Skill" or "Soft Skill."
Level 2 Category: Provides a more specific categorization of the skill.
Description: A detailed description of the skill.
Cluster Label: A value between 0 and 10 representing the group to which the skill belongs according to clustering algorithms.
Frequency: The number of times the skill appears overall in the job postings data.
Task:
Design a detailed curriculum for a Masters of Management in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning at a major North American university. The program targets students with bachelor's degrees in business, who may not have a strong technical background but are interested in applying AI and ML in industry. Some students may have more technical expertise, so the curriculum should accommodate varying levels of prior knowledge.
Key Considerations:
Skill Level Progression:
Organize skills logically from foundational to advanced.
Identify prerequisites and ensure they are taught in the appropriate order.
Learning Objectives:
Define clear learning objectives for each course.
Align courses with the overall goals of the program, ensuring each contributes meaningfully.
Skill Interdependencies:
Group related skills within the same course to reinforce learning.
Consider cross-course integration for applying skills across different courses.
Learner Engagement:
Combine theory with practical application, offering a mix of critical thinking, hands-on activities, and real-world applications.
Include a variety of learning activities (projects, case studies, collaborative work) to cater to different learning styles.
Balance of Soft and Technical Skills:
Ensure a roughly 50/50 split between technical and soft skills across the curriculum.
Consider the importance of soft skills in leadership, communication, and ethical AI deployment.
Cluster Labels and Skill Frequency:
Use cluster labels to identify and group connected skills.
Prioritize high-frequency skills that are in demand in the current job market.
Course Structure:
Design a series of 10 to 15 courses, with a maximum of 5 courses per semester.
Include elective courses to offer students flexibility.
Incorporate a capstone project to allow students to apply what they’ve learned in a practical, real-world scenario.
Industry and Accreditation Alignment:
Ensure the curriculum aligns with industry needs, accreditation requirements, and recommendations from advisory boards.
Expected Outcome: 
For each course, provide:
Course Title.
Course Description.
Exhaustive List of Skills covered in the course, specifying details such as:
Python libraries: Specify which libraries.
Machine learning algorithms: Specify which algorithms.
Leadership skills: Specify which leadership skills.
Communication skills: Specify which communication skills.
Course Division:
Organize courses by semester or into elective categories.
Include a section on course progression, explaining how topics build off each other from one semester to the next.
